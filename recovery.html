Adapt & Rebuild

Recovery

Training breaks you down. Recovery is where you actually get stronger. Without a deliberate recovery strategy, you're leaving adaptation on the table.

The Four Pillars of Recovery

Think of recovery like maintenance on a high-performance vehicle — skip it and performance degrades fast. These four pillars keep the system running.

S

Sleep

The single most important recovery tool available. During deep sleep, the body releases growth hormone, repairs muscle tissue, and consolidates motor patterns learned in training. No supplement replaces it.

  • Target 7–9 hours per night minimum
  • Prioritize sleep consistency over duration alone
  • Limit blue light and caffeine in the 2–3 hours before bed
H

Hydration

Even mild dehydration — as little as 2% body weight lost in fluid — measurably reduces strength, endurance, and cognitive performance. Electrolyte balance matters as much as total fluid intake.

  • Baseline: 0.5–1 oz of water per pound of bodyweight daily
  • Add sodium, potassium, and magnesium during heavy sweat sessions
  • Monitor urine color — pale yellow is the target
N

Nutrition Timing

The post-workout window is real. Within 60 minutes of training, your muscles are primed to absorb protein and carbohydrates for repair and glycogen replenishment. Don't skip this window.

  • 20–40g fast-digesting protein post-workout
  • Carbohydrates to restore glycogen (0.5–1g per lb bodyweight)
  • Follow with a whole-food meal within 2–3 hours

Anti-Inflammatory Nutrition

Chronic inflammation from hard training slows recovery and increases injury risk. The foods you eat every day either contribute to inflammation or work against it.

Foods That Help

Fatty fish (salmon, sardines), blueberries, tart cherry juice, turmeric, leafy greens, olive oil, and nuts all have documented anti-inflammatory properties relevant to athletic recovery.

Foods That Hurt

Ultra-processed foods, refined sugars, seed oils high in omega-6, and excessive alcohol all promote systemic inflammation — blunting the adaptation signal from training.

Tart Cherry Juice

One of the most studied recovery foods. Research shows it reduces muscle soreness and accelerates strength recovery after intense training — particularly useful during high-volume training blocks.

Recovery Strategy by Training Load

Not every session demands the same recovery effort. Match your recovery investment to the training stimulus.

Training Load Protein Target Carb Priority Key Focus
Light (cardio, mobility) 0.7g / lb bodyweight Low–Moderate Hydration, sleep quality
Moderate (strength, intervals) 0.8–0.9g / lb bodyweight Moderate–High Post-workout window, electrolytes
Heavy (max effort, high volume) 1.0g / lb bodyweight High Full refuel, 9+ hrs sleep, anti-inflammatories
Operational (field, sustained ops) 1.0g / lb bodyweight Very High Caloric surplus, electrolytes, sleep debt management
